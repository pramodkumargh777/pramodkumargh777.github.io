01Problem

Prices exist. A trustworthy view of them didn't.

Arecanut is traded through cooperative societies — MAMCOS in Shivamogga, TSS and TMS in Sirsi, TUMCOS in Channagiri — and through APMC markets reported on AGMARKNET. Each publishes on its own schedule, under its own variety names, and with different measures: a cooperative branch average is not an APMC modal price.

So the question a grower actually has — what is my crop worth today, and should I sell now or wait? — needs several fragmented sources reconciled first. Adike Naadi is designed for that reader: Kannada first, phone first.

02Users

Growers and traders across Karnataka's arecanut belt.

  • Growers deciding when and where to sell — the product covers towns from Shivamogga, Sagara and Thirthahalli to Sirsi, Puttur, Channagiri and Sringeri.
  • Traders comparing cooperative branches and APMC markets for the same variety on the same date.
  • Reach: the site works in Kannada and English; daily rates also go out through a WhatsApp channel and as short videos on social media.

03Solution

One place to read the market — without hiding the seams.

  • Latest rates by variety and town, from cooperatives and APMCs, each with its reporting date and a link to the source.
  • Compare markets — branches ranked for the same variety and the same reporting date, with CSV export.
  • Sell now or wait? — what prices did after this month in past years, net of interest, weight loss and storage costs. Explicitly “neither a forecast nor advice”.
  • Monthly trends since 2014 from 50+ markets, annotated with import-price and tax policy events, plus an anomaly review that flags one-month blips.
  • Forecast evaluation — statistical, machine-learning and foundation models backtested month by month against a simple baseline.
  • Ask a question — a grounded assistant for prices, crop care and weather (chapter 06).
  • Daily rates videos — an automated pipeline turns live API data into a short rates video with AI voice-over.
  • SEO-friendly market and variety pages, so growers searching for a town or grade land on the right rates.

04Architecture

Batch pipelines in, edge runtime out.

Scheduled Python pipelines in GitHub Actions pull cooperative rates and the government AGMARKNET API, normalise them and validate each value — outliers are checked against the recent median — before publishing to Cloudflare D1. Automated tests gate every deployment. A Hono API on Cloudflare Workers serves the React + TypeScript app and orchestrates the assistant, which calls Workers AI with the rows it has retrieved.

06Bilingual AI assistant

An assistant that shows its working.

Ask Areca Pulse answers questions about prices, markets, crop care and weather in Kannada or English. Answers are built from retrieved data, and every figure is cited — S1–S4 for price feeds, D1–D4 for documents — with chips showing how old each source is.

  • “How this answer was made” under every answer lists the data used, the assumptions, the uncertainty, the limitations, the model and the response time.
  • No invented inputs. For “Should I sell now?” it asks for quantity, commission, transport, storage, interest and weight loss — “Areca Pulse does not assume costs you have not given.”
  • Graceful degradation. When the language model is unavailable, the answer is written directly from the data, and says so.
  • Refuses to paraphrase what it can't quote. Asked in Kannada how to manage koleroga, it lists the relevant source pages instead of summarising scanned text, and points to the nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra, ICAR-CPCRI (Vittal) or taluk horticulture office.
  • Limits: 30 questions an hour; questions are not stored. Every answer ends with a disclaimer — no price guarantees, financial advice or disease diagnosis.

Under the hood it is a RAG pipeline on Workers AI — Qwen3-30B for generation and BGE-M3 embeddings for retrieval — behind the same Hono API as the rest of the product. Answer-sentence grounding checks, a retry and a data-only fallback keep unsupported claims out of answers.

08What's next

Beat the baseline, honestly.

The forecast leaderboard is the clearest open problem: by the product's own evaluation, no model yet beats “last month's price” by a margin that holds up. Closing that gap — without overstating what the models can do — is the bar for any forecast feature.
